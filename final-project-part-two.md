## Your White Christmas Might Be Closer Than You Think

*Start in Pittsburgh. Find a little winter magic beyond your doorstep.*

### Still excited when you see snow? Me too.

I grew up somewhere snow was rare. A white landscape was something I wished for, and even now, I still want to rush outside when the first flakes arrive.

If you live in Pittsburgh and feel the same way, perhaps your Christmas wish is simple: somewhere snowy, a little time outdoors, and someone to share it with.

You could wait for snow to come to you. Or you could give yourself a better chance of finding it.

### Let the map spark an idea

A white Christmas does not require snowflakes falling on Christmas morning. Here, it means **at least one inch of snow already on the ground on December 25**—enough to turn a familiar landscape into something different.

NOAA’s historical probabilities, based on 1991–2020 climate normals, show where that scene has been more likely.

The darker blues offer inspiration: many northern stations and parts of the mountainous West have higher probabilities than much of the country. But you do not need to turn this map into a search for the farthest, snowiest destination.

For those of us in Pittsburgh, the useful question is closer to home: **where could a change of scenery also bring a better chance of snow?**

### Pittsburgh is your starting point—not your only option

At Pittsburgh’s representative weather station, the historical probability of a white Christmas is **30.6%**. There is reason to hope, but also reason to explore.

The eight selected cities below offer a mix of nearby possibilities and farther-away inspiration.

Cleveland and Erie offer modest improvements, with probabilities of **39.7%** and **45.0%**. For a bigger holiday getaway, Burlington reaches **62.3%**, while Minneapolis reaches **73.6%**—43 percentage points above Pittsburgh.

A familiar holiday destination is not necessarily a snowier one: New York City’s representative station sits at just **13.2%**.

So if snow is part of the experience you want, let it help shape your destination. And before you start imagining a long trip, take a look at what is nearer.

### A little closer. A lot more possibility.

Within 300 kilometers of Pittsburgh’s reference station, the picture becomes more interesting. Some closer locations have considerably higher historical probabilities than the Cleveland station.

**Laurel Summit**, a mountain-area location about **98 kilometers** away, has a historical probability of roughly **66%**. The station at **Titusville**, about **135 kilometers** away, is at roughly **64%**.

Both are closer to the Pittsburgh reference station than the Cleveland station, yet their probabilities are substantially higher. These examples give you a reason to look beyond the most familiar city names when imagining a snowy escape.

They are not guaranteed snow destinations, or the only places worth considering. What they show is encouraging: **you do not always have to go farther to improve your chances. A thoughtfully chosen nearby location can be a promising place to start.**

*Distances shown are straight-line distances from the Pittsburgh International Airport reference station. Check actual routes and travel times when planning your trip.*

### Make room for a white Christmas

Perhaps your version is a walk through snowy trees. Perhaps it is a snowball fight with friends, a few photographs, or warming your hands around a hot drink afterward.

Use these historical odds to choose a place worth exploring. As Christmas approaches, check the local forecast, snow conditions, and your route—and make a plan you can enjoy even if the weather changes.

**This Christmas, give your wish for snow somewhere to take you. Invite a friend, bundle up, and step outside to find a little winter magic of your own.**

*Source: NOAA NCEI, Christmas Day Snow Statistics, 1991–2020 U.S. Climate Normals. Probabilities refer to at least one inch of snow on the ground on December 25 at individual weather stations. They are historical estimates, not citywide averages or forecasts for this Christmas.*

## Target audience

Pittsburgh-area students and residents who enjoy snow and are interested in a Christmas outing or trip. The story helps them explore destinations with higher historical chances of a white Christmas, especially options closer to home. The goal is to understand whether readers grasp the story’s message, find the charts and writing clear, and feel the content is relevant to them.
